Short answer: yes, 40 WPM is a good speed

40 WPM is above the ~40 WPM average for adult typists and is enough for what most people actually do: email, chat, notes, everyday office work. If your goal is a typing-heavy job, though, 40 WPM is a starting point, not a finish line.

Words per minute is standardised as five characters = one word, including spaces. So 40 WPM means about 200 characters per minute, or roughly 3.3 keystrokes per second.

Accuracy matters more than raw speed once you are past roughly 30 WPM. A 40 WPM typist at 99% accuracy finishes most real tasks faster than a 55 WPM typist at 90%, because every error costs a look-back, a correction and a re-read.

How to check if you actually type 40 WPM

  1. Take a timed test — 30 seconds is enough, 60 seconds is more stable.
  2. Type at your natural pace, not your maximum. A test you "sprint" through measures your ceiling, not your working speed.
  3. Read the accuracy number next to the WPM number. If accuracy is under 95%, your real-world speed is lower than the score suggests.
  4. Repeat on another day. Typing speed varies with keyboard, fatigue and how well you know the text.

How to get from 40 to 60 WPM

The jump from 40 to 60 is mostly about removing two habits: looking at the keyboard, and typing beyond your accuracy.

1. Stop looking at your hands

Find the home row by feel. Put your index fingers on the two keys with raised bumps and let the rest fall into place. Looking down resets your muscle memory every time — the eyes are the bottleneck, not the fingers.

2. Type slower to type faster

Set a pace you can hold at 98%+ accuracy, even if it feels slow. Speed built on errors does not transfer to real typing, because real typing requires you to be right.

3. Practise 15 minutes a day, not 2 hours once a week

Typing is a motor skill. Short daily sessions build the automaticity that long rare sessions do not.

4. Use real text

Practise on the kind of writing you actually do — code, email, prose — so the letter and word patterns you drill are the ones you need.

5. Watch accuracy, not speed, for the first month

If accuracy is 97%+ and you are not getting faster, add pace. If accuracy drops below 95%, slow down immediately. That single rule gets most people through the 40-to-60 gap.

Is 40 WPM enough for a job?

It depends on the role. General office, support, admin and most knowledge work: 40 WPM is fine. Roles where typing is the job — data entry, transcription, medical and legal typing, live captioning — commonly list 60-80 WPM as a requirement, and some employers test it at interview. If you are aiming at those, 40 WPM is your baseline and 60+ is the target.

Frequently asked

Is 40 WPM average?

Roughly, yes. A commonly cited figure for adult typists is around 40 WPM, which makes 40 WPM the middle of the pack rather than fast or slow.

Is 40 WPM fast for a beginner?

Yes. Many beginners start below 25 WPM, so 40 WPM already means you are done with hunt-and-peck.

Does 40 WPM include mistakes?

Well-reported WPM is always paired with an accuracy figure. Gross WPM counts everything you typed; net WPM subtracts errors. Our test shows both the speed and the accuracy so you can judge for yourself.

Can I reach 40 WPM without touch typing?

Some people do, using four or six fingers and glancing at the keyboard. It usually plateaus somewhere in the 35-45 range, which is another reason 40 WPM is such a common sticking point.

How many keystrokes is 40 WPM?

About 200 characters per minute, so roughly 3.3 keystrokes per second.
